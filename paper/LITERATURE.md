## Computational and AI-driven design of spinal interbody cages

_Scope: Computational and AI-driven design of spinal interbody cages: topology optimisation with bone response, quality-diversity/generative encodings, LLM program evolution, multi-agent verification, Bayesian/active-learning FE loops, GPU FE solvers, contact surrogates, spine test protocols and degradable-cage failure data relevant to BIOCAGE (PLCL C3-C4 temporary support cage)_

### Synthesis

## What the literature establishes

**Optimised cages exist, but for fusion, stiffness and subsidence, never for dislocation.** Lin et al. 2004 (two-scale TO, 50 % volume fraction, displacement constraints) and Smit et al. 2024 (full-scale TO with principal-strain constraints in the adjacent vertebrae; in-silico trial on 7 patients, median subsidence-risk reduction 89 % Ti / 94 % PEEK; ASME V&V40-2018 credibility) are the state of the art. Their "bone in the loop" is a strain constraint, not remodelling, and their materials are Ti (110 GPa) and PEEK (3 GPa). No paper optimises a resorbable, soft/hard graded cage for friction-only fixation; mu_req (EVO methods/e02, e04) is therefore a new objective and must be defined and validated carefully in the paper.

**Generative and quality-diversity design is mature in soft robotics, not in implants.** CPPN multi-material encodings (Cheney 2013), MAP-Elites (Mouret & Clune 2015), surrogate-assisted illumination (Gaier 2018: orders of magnitude fewer evaluations) and MAP-Elites + TO hybrids (Xie 2024) give the EVO designer role ready-made machinery; SAIL also reconciles the existing GP/BoTorch active learning (DECISIONS_baseline_human_c34.md:139-146) with the planned archive.

**LLM program evolution works only with a trustworthy automatic evaluator.** FunSearch (Nature 2024) and AlphaEvolve (arXiv 2506.13131) both state this precondition. Our own history shows evaluator failures propagate silently (combined-loading bug before 2026-10-26; split v1 restart LoadData bug), so evaluator validation (gate 2, sequential-vs-split checks like p062) is the critical path before a designer agent runs.

**Adversarial multi-agent verification has theory and early evidence but known weak points.** Debate (Irving 2018; Du 2024) and tournament debate in the AI co-scientist (Gottweis 2025) improve factuality; the AI Scientist (Lu 2024) documents agents filling ~1 TB with checkpoints and self-relaunching, the published analogue of our /raid-full incident. Fact-checking studies report weak LLM moderators and a negativity bias, so verifier/red-team verdicts must be settled by numeric ground truth, not by a judge model.

**Credibility frameworks are now expected.** ASME V&V40-2018, the FDA 2023 guidance and Erdemir's ten rules define context of use, risk-informed evidence and documentation; Smit 2024 shows reviewers accept them in a cage paper.

**GPU FE:** TLED (Miller 2007; NiftySim 2015, >10x, contact available) is the validated explicit route the gpufe solver follows; JAX-FEM is 10x but has no contact (METHODS_COMPARISON.md:40); FEBio+cuDSS yields 3.7x on the solve, 2.2x overall (line 39). Learned contact surrogates (SCMT on FEBio; GNN surrogates up to 1000x) do not handle Coulomb stick-slip.

**Spine protocols and degradable-cage failures:** Wilke 1998 pure moments, 1.5 N m with the 1009-segment dataset (Farber 2025: C3-4 LB 5.9 deg), Patwardhan follower load (buckling ~10 N without it), Moroney 1988 (source of 1164 N and 135 N thresholds). Stiff resorbable cages failed by creep, subsidence and osteolysis (Krijnen 2006; Smit 2010; PEEK-vs-PLDLLA RCT); the lab's PLA cages fractured and 2/4 TE-IVDs displaced in 6 weeks (Fidai 2024; Lintz 2021).

## Contested
Whether explicit TLED is adequate for quasi-static frictional contact; whether in-silico subsidence credibility transfers to migration; cage-bone friction coefficients in FE range 0.07-0.8 with no measured cervical value; the worth of LLM judges.

## Missing / implications for BIOCAGE
Must cite: Herron 2020, Maas 2012, Wilke 1998, Moroney 1988, Farber 2025, Smit 2024 (both), Lin 2004, Mouret 2015, Cheney 2013, Gaier 2018, FunSearch, AlphaEvolve, Irving 2018, V&V40/FDA, Erdemir 2020, Krijnen 2006, Smit 2010, Fidai 2024, Lintz 2021, Jeong 2004. Must measure: PLCL-endplate friction coefficient (wet, with/without cartilage endplate) to turn mu_req into a pass/fail with margin; cadaveric C3-4 push-out under 50-100 N preload (ASTM F2077 does not cover expulsion; use the draft push-out protocol, 5 mm/min, 3 mm endpoint); sustained-load creep of 85:15 and 50:50 PLCL at 37 C wet to 26 weeks; modulus-vs-time of both grades; split2 sampling-overshoot correction (4-9 % in extension/AR) by interpolating to 1.5 N m; a V&V40 context-of-use statement per rule (slip-out, compression <= 15/25 %, 1164 N capacity).

### Gaps (candidate experiments)

- No published friction coefficient for PLCL (or any smooth polymer) against a human cervical endplate under physiological normal load; FE studies assume 0.07-0.8. Experiment: pin-on-endplate and cage-on-FSU shear tests, wet, 50-150 N normal load, with and without the cartilaginous endplate, to set mu_available and its spread for the mu_req rule.
- No in vivo or in vitro modulus-vs-time data for PLCL 85:15 and 50:50 over 16-26 weeks under sustained load at 37 C (only Jeong 2004, 50:50 scaffolds to 15 weeks, mass/structure only). Experiment: tensile/compressive creep and modulus tracking in PBS at 37 C, loaded (100 N-equivalent stress) vs unloaded, sampled at 0, 2, 4, 8, 16, 26 weeks; feed the curves into a degradation sensitivity sweep of the 50 passing designs.
- No expulsion (push-out) benchmark for smooth, friction-only cervical cages: ASTM F2077 excludes expulsion; existing data are lumbar, toothed, foam-block tests. Experiment: cadaveric C3-C4 push-out (posterior and anterior) under 50-100 N follower preload at 5 mm/min to compare with e04 one_face_slip_bound and the 135 N posterior rule.
- No literature on dislocation (slip-out) as an optimisation objective for interbody cages in TO or quality-diversity design; mu_req / one-face slip bound are new metrics and need a published definition, convergence study (step sampling overshoot 4-9 %) and sensitivity to tied-vs-frictional interface (only pilots at mu 0.35 exist in human_study/proc_val).
- No validated explicit-GPU (TLED) solver for quasi-static frictional cage-bone contact with facets and ligaments; gpufe gate 2 (intact-FSU ROM vs Herron/FEBio) is still pending. Experiment: run the same 6 windows on 5 designs in gpufe and FEBio split2 and report ROM, slip bound and compression differences before any designer-agent use.
- No learned surrogate handles Coulomb stick-slip for implant-bone interfaces (SCMT and GNN surrogates are elastic/penalty contact). Candidate: train a GP or GNN on mu_req, slip bound and compression from the 150 AL points plus split2 outputs, then test SAIL-style archive illumination against new FEBio runs.
- No evidence on how a soft-pad (E 1.5-12 MPa) cage behaves after the biodisc stiffens or the pad degrades; combined biodisc maturation and PLCL degradation time course is unmodelled. Experiment: staged simulations at 0, 8, 16, 26 weeks with E_biodisc rising and E_soft falling, checking whether the fixation mechanism shifts from friction to the maturing tissue.
- No published failure/incident taxonomy for multi-agent AI design loops in engineering (AI Scientist reports anecdotes). Candidate: log every evaluator bug, invalid-data purge and resource incident of this project as a dataset (already partly in INVALID.md, INVALID_OUTPUTS_PURGED_2026-10-31.md, GOVERNANCE.md) and report it as a methods contribution.
- No head-to-head data for toothed vs smooth cervical cages on dislocation under physiological 1.5 N m motion with follower load (lumbar toothed spacer pull-out 1053 vs 234 N is from a patent). Experiment: compare a conventional toothed PEEK cage with the best BIOCAGE design in the same cadaveric FSU protocol.

## Cervical interbody cage / cervical disc arthroplasty biomechanics for BIOCAGE

_Scope: Cervical interbody cage / cervical disc arthroplasty biomechanics for BIOCAGE: migration, expulsion, subsidence (clinical rates, risk factors, mechanisms); push-out/expulsion test methods and reference values; ASTM F2077/F2267/F04 push-out draft, ISO 18192-1; follower load and pure-moment conventions; cervical ICR; FE studies of cage migration; endplate morphology and contour-matched implants; implant-bone friction coefficients; bioresorbable cage precedents._

### Synthesis

## What the literature establishes

**Test conventions.** Pure-moment flexibility testing (Panjabi 1988; Wilke 1998) with a compressive follower load (Patwardhan 2000: cervical spine carries 250 N follower load but collapses <40 N under vertical load; in vivo 120-1200 N) is the accepted in-vitro protocol; cervical implant cadaver studies use 1-2 N m plus 50-100 N (Lou 2018: 2.0 N m + 75 N; Lin 2022: 1 N m; Herron 2020 validation 1.5 N m). ISO 18192-1 cervical envelope is 50-150 N, +/-7.5/6/4 deg (confirmed via Kienle 2020). Our physio_rot (1.5 N m target, 100 N follower, ICR from Anderst 2013: C3/C4 +1.5 mm SI, -1.8 mm AP) sits inside these conventions and should be described in exactly these terms.

**Expulsion testing.** There is no finalized standard: ASTM F2077-24 explicitly excludes expulsion and bone-implant interface testing; the only method is the F04 draft F-04.25.02.02 (two polyurethane blocks, constant preload, push to 3 mm or failure). Reference values (FDA SSED via Kienle 2020): Mobi-C 142 +/- 18 N and MOVE-C 202.3 +/- 8.4 N at 100 N preload, Prodisc-C 303.9 +/- 29.6 N at 45 N preload; lumbar ALIF cages 287-392 N yield at 500 N preload (Kienle 2023); an elastomeric cervical implant >= 181 N. All of these use teeth/keels in 15 pcf foam, so they are not directly comparable with a tooth-free cage in a deformable FSU with mu 0.35; Kienle 2020 itself states expulsion tests are non-standardized. Our 135 N posterior target (EVO docs/DECISIONS.md:99) is attributed to Moroney 1988, whose abstract shows shear applied only to 39 N; the derivation must be checked against the full text before it enters the paper.

**Clinical rates and mechanisms.** Subsidence after ACDF is common (21 %, 0-83 %, >= 3 mm definition, Noordhoek 2018; 34 % of levels, MacCormick 2021), while migration/extrusion of fusion cages is rare (0.6-0.9 %; hardware failure 2.2 %, Rai 2024). For non-fusing cervical implants migration is the dominant complaint (25.2 % of 1347 MAUDE reports, Virk 2021). Quantified risk factors: endplate injury (OR 18.7), posterior position (OR 6.45), pear-shaped disc (OR 8.28), osteoporosis (OR 7.86) for lumbar retropulsion (Park 2019); anterior cage placement (52 % vs 25 %) and kyphosis for cervical subsidence (MacCormick 2021); over-distraction >= 2 mm raises subsidence 47 % vs 31 % (Hsueh 2023) - supporting our d <= 1.5 mm. The lab's own precedent is decisive: PLA cages fractured within 4 weeks and TE-IVD constructs extruded anteriorly within 2 weeks in minipigs (Fidai 2024; Koga 2023 FE: anterior mis-placement raises migration and corner stress). Early (< 2 weeks), anterior, rigid-body expulsion is therefore the failure mode to design against, which matches our window-based no-slip objective.

**Friction and geometry.** Measured implant-bone friction: porous metals 0.3-1.3 (Rancourt 1990), > 0.9 (Dammak 1997), 0.37-0.95 depending on texture and BMD (Abedi 2025); polished surfaces much lower and stress-independent (Damm 2015); bone-on-bone 0.58-0.91. No measurement exists for PLCL (or any PLA-family polymer) against wet bone; FE studies assume 0.07-0.8. Our mu 0.3 (0.2/0.45) is plausible for a smooth polymer but unmeasured. Cervical endplates are asymmetric (C3 inferior 2.1-2.7 mm deep, C4 superior 0.9-1.2 mm, apex posterior; Chen 2013), endplate yield is ~139 N with posterior/lateral regions strongest (Ordway 2007), and contour matching raises contact area ~74 % and halves contact stress (lumbar cadaver, Fernandes 2022). Contour-matched cervical implants have never been tested for migration or contact area (Lou 2018 measured ROM/IDP only).

## Contested / missing
Subsidence thresholds (2 vs 3 vs 4 mm) and PEEK-vs-Ti, zero-profile-vs-plate effects are inconsistent across meta-analyses; endplate injury as a migration driver is disputed in MIS cohorts; FE friction inputs span an order of magnitude. Missing entirely: cervical migration incidence with an mm threshold, polymer-bone friction, PLCL cage degradation/strength retention over 16-26 weeks, and any no-fixation (no fusion, no ingrowth) long-term stability data.

## Bearing on BIOCAGE
Cite: Patwardhan, Wilke, Panjabi, Anderst/Amevo, Herron, ISO 18192-1, F2077/F2267 and the F04 draft, Kienle 2020 (reference lines + non-comparability caveat), Noordhoek, Park, Hsueh, Chen, Ordway, Fidai/Koga/Lintz, Wuisman, Rancourt/Damm/Abedi. Measure/report: mu_req per window with real-friction confirmation; push-out in both directions at 100 N preload in a foam-block-equivalent setup for comparability AND in the FSU; cage-centre translation over 3 cycles; placement-offset robustness (0-1 mm anterior, Koga/MacCormick); endplate contact area and peak pressure vs the ~139 N / 156 N/mm yield data; post-op disc-height change vs d; the 'required friction coefficient' as a novel design metric must be validated by a bench friction measurement.

### Gaps (candidate experiments)

- No measured friction coefficient for PLCL (85:15 or 50:50), or any PLA-family polymer, against wet cervical endplate bone. Candidate experiment: inclined-plane/pin-on-disc test per Abedi 2025 design (human or porcine cancellous/endplate, 0.5-1.5 MPa, smooth vs printed micro-textured PLCL, SBF-wet) to replace the assumed mu 0.3 (0.2/0.45).
- No standardized push-out method exists (ASTM F2077-24 excludes expulsion; F04 draft only). Candidate: bench push-out of printed BIOCAGE prototypes in 15 pcf PU blocks, 100 N preload, both directions, 3 mm criterion, to put our values on the same axis as Mobi-C 142 N / MOVE-C 202 N / Prodisc-C 304 N; report FSU-FE values separately.
- No cervical cadaver or in vivo data on migration of a contour-matched (endplate-conforming) implant; Lou 2018 measured only ROM/IDP, Fernandes 2022 is lumbar contact area. Candidate: C3-C4 cadaver FSU test (2.0 N m + 75-100 N follower, 3 cycles, optical cage tracking, pressure film) comparing family A vs B.
- No population statistics of 3D C3-inferior / C4-superior endplate geometry beyond midsagittal depth (Chen 2013); our contour family is derived from one 26-year-old subject (Herron). Candidate: CT morphometry (n >= 50) to parametrize concavity depth, apex position and transverse curvature for robustness sweeps.
- No in vivo or in vitro data on PLCL cage strength retention, creep and modulus loss over the 16-26 week support window under cyclic physiological load; PLDLLA precedents crept/fractured at 3 months. Candidate: 37 C PBS cyclic creep test of hard/soft PLCL specimens and whole cages to 26 weeks, feeding time-dependent moduli into the FE.
- The 135 N posterior push-out requirement (EVO docs/DECISIONS.md:99, attributed to Moroney 1988) is not traceable from the Moroney abstract (shear applied only to 39 N). Candidate: re-derive the posterior shear demand from Moroney full text or in vivo shear estimates and document the derivation.
- No bone-side subsidence criterion in the pipeline (e05 is a PLCL strain rule; F2267 is foam-only). Candidate: map predicted endplate contact pressure against Ordway 2007 yield (139 +/- 99 N, 156 +/- 52 N/mm, posterior/lateral strongest) and add an endplate-yield output to e05.
- No data on cage stability in the ligament states expected clinically (ALL resected, PLL intact vs resected) for a no-fixation device; our model tie/friction choice and ligament state should be tested as a sensitivity (candidate FE sub-study plus cadaver pilot).
- No published cervical migration incidence with an explicit mm threshold for stand-alone cages (only 0.6-0.9 % slippage/extrusion from mixed cohorts); the paper should state this absence and define its own 1 mm / 0.5 mm posterior criteria (PLAN.md:35) as proposals.
- Early (< 2 weeks) in vivo kinetics of anterior extrusion seen in Fidai 2024 are not linked to any mechanical measurement. Candidate: instrumented minipig pilot (weekly radiographic cage-centre tracking) to calibrate the FE mu_req prediction against observed slip timing.

## Bioresorbable polymer spinal cages and load-bearing implants (PLA/PLGA/PLDLLA/PLCL)

_Scope: Bioresorbable polymer spinal cages and load-bearing implants (PLA/PLGA/PLDLLA/PLCL): clinical and animal outcomes, hydrolytic degradation and time-dependent failure at 37 C, PLCL viscoelasticity and FDM printing, polymer-bone friction_

### Synthesis

## What the literature establishes

1. **Lactide polymers fail under sustained load long before their short-term strength predicts.** 70/30 PLDLLA goat cages designed for >= 6 months failed at 3 months; static loading at 75 % of short-term strength fails in < 5 min, ~50 % within 1 day, and extrapolation puts 25 % at ~3 months, with strength lower at 37 C, wet, and at slow rates (Smit 2008; Engels 2010; Smit 2010). The mechanism is stress-activated plastic flow (Eyring), common to PLLA, PLDLLA and PDLLA. The BIOCAGE screen (quasi-static 1.5 N m windows, 100 N follower, e05 strain rule on the soft phase only) has **no criterion for sustained stress in the hard PLCL 85:15**; this is the single biggest validity gap relative to the literature.

2. **Clinical bioresorbable cages migrated, subsided and caused osteolysis.** TLIF with PLDLA: 18.2 % migration and 18.2 % nonunion vs 0 % with carbon fiber, mean failure at 9.3 months (Smith 2010); RCT PLIF: fusion 50 % vs 92 % PEEK, more subsidence (p = 0.0414), osteolysis in 2-3/12 (Jiya 2009, 2011); 4/9 osteolysis in a PLIF series (Frost 2012). Sheep C3/C4 PLDLLA: large early osteolysis, foreign-body reactions in all fusion areas (Kandziora 2004). Slowly degrading semicrystalline PLLA, by contrast, caused only mild inflammation over 4 years in goats (van Dijk 2002, 2005), and a composite beta-TCP/PLLA cervical cage reached 96 % fusion with resorption complete at ~36 months (Debusscher 2009). Human cervical PLDLLA spacer series are small and all plated (Vaccaro 2004 n = 8; Lanman 2004 n = 20); **no stand-alone, friction-fixed bioresorbable cervical cage has been reported in humans**, which is BIOCAGE's novelty and its risk.

3. **PLCL property/degradation map.** Modulus rises from 12 to 1343 MPa between 66 and 90 mol % LA, with aging/phase separation increasing with CL content (Fernandez 2012). Random CL-rich PLCL loses half its molecular weight in 18-32 days at 37 C in PBS, crystallizing and embrittling as it degrades (Fernandez 2013, venue unverified); CL-rich amorphous domains degrade first in vivo (Jeong 2004). Printed PLCL (Duan 2021): E 3.7 MPa, stress-relaxation t1/2 171 s at 20 % strain, printable at 185 C; PLCL/PLLA blends jump from ~5 MPa (20 % PLLA) to 521 MPa (30 % PLLA), i.e. a **mixing-nozzle gradient is a step, not a ramp**; h14's material map should be checked against this (not verified here).

4. **Friction.** No wet bone-vs-polylactide friction measurement was found. Implant-cancellous-bone mu spans 0.3-1.3 and is governed by asperity ploughing and lubrication (Rancourt 1990, unverified title; Grant 2007: normal stress 0.25-1.0 MPa protocol; Voutat 2019: load/speed/material/finish, values not retrieved). The pilot mu = 0.35 is an assumption, not a datum.

5. **Baselines for the paper.** ACDF subsidence 21.1 % (>= 3 mm) with rigid cages (Noordhoek 2018); cervical follower load validated to 250 N, in vivo 120-1200 N (Patwardhan 2000), so 100 N is a lower bound. ASTM F2077 excludes expulsion, so the slip-out claim needs a bespoke test. Lintz 2021: one-piece FDM PLA cages lost 13.6-39.7 % DHI in 4 weeks and all fractured posteriorly.

## Contested / missing
Whether resorbable cages per se cause osteolysis is contested; the data separate by degradation rate and crystallinity (PLDLLA bad, PLLA and composites acceptable; Mistro 2025 finds no inferiority in animals). Missing entirely: creep, compression set and 37 C-wet degradation of PLCL 50:50 and 85:15 as printed; PLCL friction on bone; any PLCL load-bearing spinal device (one unverified VU-Amsterdam record used PLCL only as a filler inside PEEK cages, 53 % vs 63 % fusion in goats).

## Bearing on BIOCAGE
Design: avoid amorphous, fast-hydrolyzing formulations in load paths; treat the soft pad as a component that may lose stiffness within weeks and verify fixation with the pad degraded (mu_req recomputed with soft E reduced). Protocol: add (i) an Eyring/Smit-type sustained-stress criterion for the hard phase (e.g. peak hard-phase stress <= 25 % of wet 37 C yield, or fitted lifetime >= 26 weeks), (ii) 250 N follower sensitivity, (iii) a degradation-time sweep of material properties (Vieira 2011 approach). Paper: cite Smit/Engels, Kandziora, Thomas, Smith, Jiya, Frost, van Dijk, Fernandez 2012/2013, Jeong, Duan, Noordhoek, Patwardhan, Lintz; measure yield and creep-rupture of printed PLCL 85:15 (3 rates, 37 C wet), compression set and relaxation of PLCL 50:50, mu vs wet cancellous bone at 0.25-1 MPa, and 0-26-week aging of printed prototypes (mass, Mw, E, mu, height).

## Tissue-engineered intervertebral discs and the need for temporary support (integrator, 2026-10-31)

### Synthesis

The Cornell / Weill Cornell programme (Bonassar, Hartl) established composite tissue-engineered discs (collagen annulus around an alginate nucleus): in the rat caudal spine they produced matrix, maintained disc height and restored dynamic mechanical function over 6 months (Bowles 2011); in the beagle cervical spine, stably implanted constructs retained disc height and hydration and integrated with host tissue over 16 weeks without immune reaction (Moriguchi 2017). The Penn programme (Mauck, Smith, Gullbrand) reached native compressive properties after 20 weeks in rats and maintained composition and structure for 8 weeks in the goat cervical spine with endplate-modified DAPS (Gullbrand 2018). Retention in the disc space is the recurring weakness: in canine cervical motion segments the construct alone did not restore stiffness and a resorbable 85:15 PLGA plate-and-screw system was needed to keep it in place (Mojica-Santiago 2018); in minipigs half of the constructs supported by flexible PLA cages extruded anteriorly within two weeks, while rigid PLA cages fractured (Fidai 2024; Lintz 2021). These papers define the BIOCAGE requirement directly: a resorbable support that fixes the construct for the maturation period (>= 16 weeks) without plates or screws, and the early (< 2 weeks) anterior extrusion as the failure to design against.

### Gaps

- No published fixation strategy for a TE disc that avoids plates/screws; BIOCAGE is the first (novelty claim to state carefully).
- Maturation-time data exist for rat (20-24 weeks to native stiffness), dog (16 weeks integration) and goat (8 weeks); the human time course is unknown, so the 16-26 week support window is an assumption to state as such.
- No data on how a maturing disc shares load with a resorbable support; staged simulations (biodisc E rising, PLCL E falling) are needed.

## Credibility, validation and reporting practice (integrator, 2026-10-31)

### Synthesis

The accepted framework for a device-simulation paper is ASME V&V 40-2018 with the FDA 2023 guidance (see comp_design sweep): state the context of use and question of interest (here: no dislocation, soft-phase compression, load capacity for a temporary cervical support), rank model risk, and set credibility goals per activity (code verification, calculation verification, validation against comparators, uncertainty quantification). The spine FE literature has established validation data sets: Panjabi 2001 load-displacement curves, Wheeldon 2008 young-normal lower cervical FE validation, Herron 2020 (our source model, validated against Panjabi and facet-force data), and the 1009-segment 1.5 N m data set of Farber 2025. Reporting guidelines (Hicks 2015; Erdemir 2020 ten rules) ask for explicit limitations, version control and sharing; the DECISIONS.md log, the h24 preflight and the invalid-run registers (INVALID.md) already implement several of the rules and should be presented as such. What the project still lacks for a credibility assessment: a formal context-of-use statement, uncertainty quantification over the material ranges (the 3 x 3 sensitivity exists but is not yet summarised as UQ), a mesh-convergence statement for the split2 protocol, and validation of the tied-interface friction shortcut against the real-friction pilots (gate 4).
